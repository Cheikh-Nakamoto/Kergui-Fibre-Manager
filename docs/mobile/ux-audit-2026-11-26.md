# UX audit of the Kergui mobile app — 2026-11-26

Scope: every route in `mobile/src/app/`, the shared components in `mobile/src/ui/`, the French
strings in `mobile/src/i18n/fr.ts`, the WhatsApp and push texts written by the hub
(`internal/hub/`), and the design of [RFC-007](../rfcs/RFC-007-closed-wifi.md) (Wi-Fi fermé,
ticket #108). Read-only: no app code was changed.

Audience assumed throughout: Senegalese households, not technical, often without a PC, French as a
second language for many (Wolof is the everyday language), mid-range Android phones, patchy
connectivity. Where a claim rests on my judgment and not on a source, it says so.

Method: code reading (each finding cites `path:line`), a contrast computation on the theme's real
colours, and the sources listed in § 5. Nothing was run on a device; see "Not checked" at the end.

---

## 1. Summary — the five most important problems

1. **The owner can cut their own phone off the box** (turning off the Wi-Fi it is on, « Couper l'accès » on this phone from the Conso tab, or changing that Wi-Fi's password), and no screen says so before it happens.
2. **On WhatsApp, « 2 » means "approve" in a one-device alert and "block device n° 2" in a multi-device alert**, and an unquoted reply goes to the newest alert: a learned habit can block the wrong device.
3. **The « Filtrage MAC » switch** shows a banned word, explains the opposite of what the box does, is red in its normal state, and turning it off silently gives internet back to every blocked device.
4. **Lost phone after the forced first password**: the new box password lives only in that phone (« Kergui le gardera pour vous »), and both reconnecting and « Récupérer à la maison » need it (plus a relay IP address). A one-phone household ends at a box reset.
5. **Too many words for the same two actions**: approve / accept / autoriser / nouveau / inconnu, and bloquer / couper l'accès / couper Internet / refusé / rejet. The device sheet shows two "block" buttons and can show two "approve" buttons at once.

### C1. The owner can lock their own phone out — Critical

**Where**
- Turning a Wi-Fi network off: `mobile/src/app/(tabs)/wifi.tsx:42-49`, text `fr.ts:182`
  « {action} le réseau « {name} » ? Les appareils connectés dessus seront coupés. »
- This phone's device sheet, reached from the Conso tab: `mobile/src/lib/usage.ts:25-43` builds rows
  from **all** devices (no `isHidden` filter, unlike `devices.tsx:125`), and each row opens the sheet
  (`(tabs)/usage.tsx` `Row`, `router.push('/device/[mac]')`). The sheet never checks `Mark === 'Self'`
  or `'Owner'` (`mobile/src/app/device/[mac].tsx:52-91`, `133-144`).
- Changing the password of the Wi-Fi this phone uses: `wifi.tsx:73-86`, `fr.ts:192-195`.

**What the user experiences.** The phone is on the box's Wi-Fi to talk to it. Turning that network
off, or « Couper l'accès » on this very phone, drops the phone off the Wi-Fi. « Activer » and
« Rendre l'accès » are free forever (RFC-006), but the app can no longer reach the box to run them.
The household needs a cable, another device, or the box's web page. The confirm text only says
"devices connected to it" and never "including this phone". After a password change the
success line `wifi.passwordChanged` (`wifi.tsx:177-181`) is often never seen, because the phone has
just been disconnected, and nothing tells the user to type the new password on this phone.

**Evidence.** The core marks this phone (`mobilecore/approvals.go:383`, `MarkSelf`) only for display.
No local guard exists: `MarkSelf` is used nowhere else. The relay has one for the same case
(`internal/agent/house.go:115-242`, `ErrSelfHarm` refuses to cut the network the relay is on), the
phone does not. The list hides this phone (`devices.tsx:121-126`), so the sheet is reachable only by
the side door of the Conso tab, which makes it more surprising.

**Research.** Nielsen's error-prevention heuristic and NN/g's guidance that confirmations must name
the specific consequence ([NN/g, Confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)).
WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.4 Error Prevention asks that consequential actions be reversible, checked or
confirmed ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html)).
An action the app cannot reverse by itself is the case these rules exist for.

**Recommendation.**
- In the core (so the relay, WhatsApp and the app share it): refuse, with a dedicated code, cutting
  Wi-Fi access of the `Self` device, and turning off the last enabled network or the network `Self`
  is on (its `SSID` is known from the device list). Mirror `ErrSelfHarm`.
- On the sheet of `Self` / `Owner` devices: hide « Bloquer » / « Couper l'accès » and show
  « C'est ce téléphone : Kergui ne le bloque jamais. »; filter hidden devices out of the Conso rows,
  or show them with that line.
- On the network this phone uses: « Ce téléphone est connecté à ce réseau. Si vous l'éteignez,
  Kergui ne pourra plus parler à la box : il faudra un câble pour le rallumer. » with the switch
  disabled when it is the last network.
- Wi-Fi password of this phone's network: say before confirming « Ce téléphone va se déconnecter.
  Reconnectez-le avec le nouveau mot de passe, puis rouvrez Kergui. » (see H8).

### C2. WhatsApp « 2 » approves or blocks, depending on the alert — Critical

**Where.** `internal/hub/alerts/usecase/alert.go:220-225`,
`internal/hub/replyblock/replyblock.go:181` and `204-206`,
`internal/hub/alerts/infrastructure/postgres.go:172-197`, fallback text
`internal/hub/alerts/handler/handler.go:240`.

**What the user experiences.**
- A one-device alert says « Répondez 1 pour bloquer cet appareil, ou 2 pour l'approuver ».
- A two-device alert says « Répondez par le numéro de l'appareil à bloquer (par exemple « 1 », ou
  « 1 2 »), ou OK pour les approuver tous ».
- A person who learned "2 = c'est bon" from the first kind blocks device n° 2 with the second kind.
- If they do not quote the message, the reply goes to the **newest** open alert
  (`postgres.go:188-197`), which may not be the one they are reading.
- The "I did not understand" text then says « OK pour l'approuver », a third convention.

**Evidence.** `replyblock.go:181`: `isTwo(intent) && len(alert.Devices) == 1` approves; otherwise the
2 is a device number and goes to `blockOne`.

**Research.** Consistency and standards is one of Nielsen's ten heuristics
([NN/g, 10 heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)). Novice and
low-literacy users rely on memorised sequences more than on reading each screen; Medhi et al.
found that most of 90 low-literacy subjects could not use hierarchical menus and recalled
step sequences instead ([Medhi et al., ToCHI 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf), § 3).
WhatsApp is the main channel in Senegal ([DataReportal, Digital 2025: Senegal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-senegal)),
so this text is read more often than most app screens.

**Recommendation.**
- One meaning per token, always: digits are device numbers to block, « OK » is approve.
- Single device: « Répondez 1 pour le bloquer, ou OK pour l'autoriser. »
- Several devices: « Répondez le numéro de l'appareil à bloquer (1, 2…), ou OK pour tous les
  autoriser. »
- Keep accepting a bare « 2 » on a one-device alert for a transition period, but stop teaching it.
- When a reply is not quoted and more than one alert is open, answer first: « Pour quelle alerte ?
  Répondez directement au message de l'alerte (appui long, puis Répondre). » instead of acting.
- Align with RFC-007 D9 « OK » (see § 3, R11).

### C3. « Filtrage MAC »: banned word, wrong meaning, silent mass unblock — Critical

**Where.** `mobile/src/app/(tabs)/wifi.tsx:204-244`, `fr.ts:115`, `fr.ts:175-177`;
setup at first connection `mobile/src/session/filterSetup.ts:9-17` (called from `connect.tsx:90`),
`internal/usecase/macfilter.go:77-108`, `internal/adapter/controller/httpapi/server.go:435-479`.

**What the user experiences.**
- At the top of the Wi-Fi tab, a switch titled « Filtrage MAC », coloured **red** when on
  (`wifi.tsx:237-238`).
- Kergui itself turned it on, in blocklist mode, at the first connection (`filterSetup.ts`). So the
  normal, healthy state looks like an alarm.
- Its confirm text says « Activer le filtrage MAC ? Seuls les appareils autorisés pourront se
  connecter. », which describes allowlist mode, the opposite of what the box is in (blocklist,
  `macfilter.go:77-80`).
- Turning it off asks « Désactiver le filtrage MAC ? » and nothing else. It gives internet back, at
  once, to **every** device the household blocked. It is free (`restore_wifi_access`), so even a
  child on a lapsed pass can do it.
- « filtrage MAC » is on the banned list for customer text, and it appears twice (`fr.ts:115`,
  `fr.ts:175-177`).

**Research.** Plain-language and cognitive-accessibility guidance: remove or explain jargon, and do
not give familiar words new meanings ([W3C COGA, Use clear words](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o3p01-clear-words)).
NN/g's toggle guidance: the label must make clear what each state does, or users second-guess it
([NN/g, Toggle-switch guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/toggle-switch-guidelines/)).

**Recommendation.**
- Remove the switch from the household's screen. Kergui manages the filter itself (it already sets
  it up). If a way to undo everything is needed, make it explicit: « Débloquer tous les appareils
  bloqués ({n}) », with the list shown before confirming.
- If the switch must stay for support, move it to the technical journal area under « Réglages
  avancés », worded « Blocages actifs sur la box » (on = green, normal).
- Off-confirm text: « Les {n} appareils bloqués retrouveront internet tout de suite. Continuer ? »
- Fix `fr.ts:115` too: « Les blocages se règlent à la maison, sur le Wi-Fi de la box. »

### C4. Lost phone: the box password Kergui chose is gone with it — Critical

**Where.** `mobile/src/ui/BoxFirstPassword.tsx:65` showing `fr.ts:33-34`
(« Kergui le gardera pour vous, et vous le retrouverez dans Réglages. »); `connect.tsx:179-186`;
`mobile/src/ui/Recovery.tsx:127-142`; `fr.ts:424-426`; `BoxPasswordSetting.tsx:38` (owner only).

**What the user experiences.**
- A new F6600P forces a new admin password (#93). Kergui tells the owner it will remember it for
  them, which invites them not to write it down.
- If the phone is lost or stolen, the new phone needs that password twice: to connect to the box
  (`connect.tsx`) and for « Récupérer à la maison ».
- « Récupérer à la maison » also asks for « Adresse du relais sur le Wi-Fi (ex. 192.168.1.20) »,
  to be found « dans l'onglet Appareils … ou dans le journal du PC relais » (`fr.ts:425-426`).
- In a one-phone household nobody else has it. The way out is a factory reset of the box, which
  also wipes the Wi-Fi name and password, and on a fibre box may need the operator.

**Evidence.**
- The password is stored only in the core's vault and the relays receive it sealed
  (`fr.ts:469`, `fr.ts:510`). Neither can show it.
- The Settings item that reveals it is on the lost phone (`settings.tsx:95`).
- The hub backup carries names, notes and consumption, not the password (`fr.ts:572`, `fr.ts:583`).

**Research.** GSMA counts lack of digital skills and literacy as the second barrier to mobile
internet use in sub-Saharan Africa
([GSMA, State of Mobile Internet Connectivity 2024](https://gsma.com/r/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/The-State-of-Mobile-Internet-Connectivity-Report-2024.pdf)).
A recovery path that needs an IP address and a password the app said not to worry about will fail
for this audience. The point about phone theft rates is my judgment.

**Recommendation.**
- At the forced first password, add one mandatory step: « Notez ce mot de passe et gardez-le hors du
  téléphone : il vous servira si vous perdez ce téléphone. » with three choices:
  - « Je l'ai écrit » (checkbox),
  - « L'envoyer sur WhatsApp à un proche » (share sheet, pre-filled),
  - « L'afficher pour le photographier ».
- Replace « Kergui le gardera pour vous » with « Kergui le garde aussi sur ce téléphone. »
- Offer a generated password that is easy to read aloud and write down (words and digits, no
  look-alike characters) as the default.
- « Récupérer à la maison »: find the relay on the LAN automatically (it is on the same network and
  answers the relay protocol) and keep the address field only as a fallback under « Je ne le trouve
  pas ».
- Add a « J'ai perdu mon téléphone » entry on the welcome screen (`app/index.tsx`) that explains the
  ways back before asking for anything.

### H1. Four words and two buttons for "block" — High

**Where.** Device sheet `mobile/src/app/device/[mac].tsx:243-267`; list sections
`(tabs)/devices.tsx:237-248`; house card `mobile/src/ui/HouseInternet.tsx:39-62`; strings below.

**What the user experiences.** The same sheet has a red « Bloquer » and, below it, « Couper
l'accès ». They do different things that this audience cannot tell apart: internet off vs refused
on the Wi-Fi. The words also vary from screen to screen:

| Concept | Words used today | Where |
|---|---|---|
| No internet for one device | « Bloquer », « Bloqué », « Bloqués par le filtre » | `fr.ts:67`, `fr.ts:70`, `fr.ts:149` |
| Refused on the Wi-Fi | « Couper l'accès », « Accès coupé », « refusé au Wi-Fi », « Rejet automatique » | `fr.ts:68-69`, `fr.ts:155-162`, `fr.ts:291`, `fr.ts:309` |
| Every device at once | « Couper Internet » | `fr.ts:119` |
| Giving back | « Débloquer », « Rendre l'accès », « Rétablir Internet » | `fr.ts:150`, `fr.ts:159`, `fr.ts:120` |

**Research.**
- Many users do not separate "Wi-Fi" from "internet". The best-known evidence is LIRNEasia's
  surveys, where Facebook users reported not using the internet
  ([LIRNEasia 2014](https://lirneasia.net/2014/08/more-facebook-users-than-internet-users-in-south-east-asia/);
  [Quartz, 2015](https://qz.com/333313)). Applying this to Wi-Fi vs internet in Senegal is my
  inference, worth testing.
- Do not give one thing several names ([W3C COGA, Use clear words](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o3p01-clear-words)).

**Recommendation.**
- One verb pair everywhere: « Bloquer » / « Débloquer ». One state: « Bloqué ».
- The consequence goes in a sub-line, not in a second button: « Bloqué : plus d'internet sur cet
  appareil. »
- The Wi-Fi refusal becomes an option of the block, exactly as RFC-007 D5 plans (« Le déconnecter
  aussi du Wi-Fi »). Do it now: merge `devices.cutSection` and `devices.blockedSection` into one
  « Bloqués ({n}) » list.
- House card: « Bloquer toute la maison » / « Tout débloquer ».

### H2. Approve, accept, new, unknown: three concepts for one decision — High

**Where.** `fr.ts:65` (« Nouveau »), `fr.ts:163-171` (« En attente d'acceptation »,
« Accepter cet appareil », « Approuvé », « Non approuvé », « Approuver »), `fr.ts:218-236`,
`fr.ts:305-318`, `fr.ts:333-334` (« appareil inconnu »); sheet `device/[mac].tsx:149-210`.

**What the user experiences.**
- In the list, a device carries « Nouveau » (`NewlySeen`).
- On its sheet it is « Non approuvé » with an « Approuver » button. When auto-reject runs, it is
  **also** « En attente d'acceptation » with an « Accepter cet appareil » button, on the same screen.
  Both write the same approval (RFC-007 D1 says so).
- Notifications say « Appareil non approuvé ». Settings and WhatsApp say « appareil inconnu ».
- « Lui donner un nom l'approuve aussi » (`fr.ts:169`) adds a hidden rule.

**Research.** Same as H1: one word per concept, no hidden rules
([W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/)). NN/g on consistency
([10 heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)).

**Recommendation.**
- Adopt RFC-007's words now, before #108: « Autoriser » / « Autorisé » / « À vérifier ».
- Replace « Approuver », « Accepter cet appareil », « Non approuvé », « inconnu ».
- Show one block on the sheet. When auto-reject is pending, put the deadline in it:
  « À vérifier · sera refusé à 14:32 si personne ne l'autorise » with one button « Autoriser ».
- Make the rename rule visible after renaming: « Nommé et autorisé ✓ ».

### H3. Raw technical errors reach the screen — High

**Where.** `mobile/src/i18n/index.ts:40` and `:42` (`error.generic` « Erreur : {error} » with the Go
message), `:50-51` (« La box a refusé la commande (code {status}). », `fr.ts:108`), `:70`;
`BoxFirstPassword.tsx:114` (« Réponse de la box : {reason} », `fr.ts:52`); `fr.ts:442`,
`fr.ts:580`, `fr.ts:588`, `fr.ts:593` (« … a échoué : {error} »).

**What the user experiences.** Any error the mapping does not know shows the core's English or Go
text, an HTTP status, or the box's own message.

**Research.**
- Error messages should use plain language, explain the problem and suggest a fix, with codes
  hidden or kept for support ([NN/g, Error-message guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)).
- WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.3 Error Suggestion ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-suggestion.html)).

**Recommendation.**
- Replace `error.generic` with « Ça n'a pas marché. Réessayez. Si ça continue : Réglages >
  Signaler un problème (le détail est dans le journal). », and send the raw text to `core.note`
  only.
- Give the « Signaler » step as a button inside the message, not as text (see M3).
- `remote.error.box`: « La box n'a pas accepté. Réessayez dans une minute. »

### H4. Locked actions look available until tapped — High

**Where.** `mobile/src/licensing/useGate.ts:27` (`Alert.alert('', t('pro.locked'))`); device sheet
buttons `device/[mac].tsx:243-254`; Wi-Fi tab `wifi.tsx:42-86`; compare `FeatureTile.tsx:21-34`
(shows a lock and « Niveau Basic ») and the Conso arrow (`usage.tsx`, icon `lock`).

**What the user experiences.**
- On Free, « Bloquer » is a full red button.
- Tapping it opens an alert with no title and one sentence, « Cette fonctionnalité n'est pas
  disponible pour votre compte. », and « OK ».
- The same feature is shown locked with its level on « Plus de fonctionnalités », so the app is
  inconsistent with itself.
- The person tried to protect their home and was refused after the fact. That is a trust cost.

**Research.**
- System status should be visible before acting
  ([NN/g, 10 heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), #1).
- Icons need a visible label ([NN/g, Icon usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)).
- Google Play forbids steering to other payment methods inside the app
  ([Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)).
  So the fix must stay neutral: it shows a state, never an offer.

**Recommendation.**
- Show the lock on the control itself, as the Conso arrow already does: outlined button, lock icon,
  label « Bloquer » with the line « Pas disponible pour votre compte » under it, read in full by
  the screen reader (`accessibilityHint`).
- Keep the neutral sentence. Add no price, no link and no « Max » call to action, which the bundle
  guard would also reject.
- Give the alert a title (« Bloquer ») so it is not a bare sentence.

### H5. Devices are named by MAC, IP and hostname — High

**Where.** Rows `(tabs)/devices.tsx:37` (« {IP} · {MAC} »); names `mobile/src/lib/format.ts:6-8`
(custom name, else hostname, else MAC); sheet header text `fr.ts:173`
(« MAC … IP … Fabricant … Wi-Fi … ») shown first (`device/[mac].tsx:215-222`); sheet title is the MAC
for an unlisted device (`device/[mac].tsx:109`); auto-reject list `AutoRejectSetting.tsx:102`
(`description={d.MAC}`).

**What the user experiences.** The question this app answers is "whose device is this?". The list
answers with « android-… », « 192.168.1.x · xx:xx:… ». Since recent Android and iOS randomise the
address per network ([Android, MAC randomization](https://source.android.com/docs/core/connect/wifi-mac-randomization-behavior)),
the vendor is often missing too, so even a technician cannot tell.

**Research.**
- Use visual cues and icons for low-literacy users
  ([Medhi et al. 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf),
  recommendation 1; [Google Design, Connectivity, culture and credit](https://design.google/library/connectivity-culture-and-credit)).
- Explain or remove jargon ([W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o3p01-clear-words)).

**Recommendation.**
- Row: a device-type icon (phone, TV, computer, unknown) from vendor or hostname hints.
- Title: the custom name, else a readable guess (« Téléphone Samsung », « Appareil sans nom »).
- Sub-line: « Connecté depuis 10 min » or « Vu hier à 21 h ».
- Move MAC, IP and Wi-Fi to a closed « Détails techniques » section at the bottom of the sheet.
- Put « Donnez-lui un nom » with a text field at the top of the sheet for unnamed devices.
- Add « Adresse privée : peut changer » under the name, as RFC-007 D10 plans.

### H6. First connection asks an unanswerable certificate question — High

**Where.**
- `mobile/src/app/connect.tsx:15-22`, `fr.ts:25-28`: « Votre box utilise un certificat qu'elle a
  signé elle-même. Kergui va retenir son empreinte… » followed by a hex fingerprint.
- Cancel: `connect.tsx:48-52` and `:66`. It returns `null` and no message.
- Fields: `connect.tsx:163-186` (address, identifier, password with no "show" eye).

**What the user experiences.** In the first minute, a dialog about a self-signed certificate and a
fingerprint nobody can check. « Faire confiance » is the only way forward, so it trains people to
click through. « Annuler » ends silently: the button stops spinning and nothing is said.
The connect screen also shows « Adresse de la box » and « Identifiant » fields. They are pre-filled
correctly, so most users should never touch them. The box label's password is long and random,
and it cannot be shown while typing.

**Research.**
- Habituation: frequent or unexplained confirmations get clicked through
  ([NN/g, Confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)).
- Reduce text input and complexity for novice users
  ([Medhi et al. 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf),
  recommendation 5).

**Recommendation.**
- Trust on first use without a dialog when the address is the default private one, and journal it.
- Keep a dialog only when the fingerprint changes (`error.pin`, already well worded).
- If a dialog stays: title « C'est bien votre box ? », body « Kergui va la reconnaître la prochaine
  fois. », buttons « Oui, c'est ma box » / « Non ». Hide the hex behind « Détails ».
- On « Non », say: « Connexion annulée. Vérifiez que vous êtes sur le Wi-Fi de votre box. »
- Fold address and identifier into « Paramètres avancés ».
- Add an eye icon to every password field (`connect.tsx`, `BoxFirstPassword.tsx:69-77`,
  `wifi.tsx:141-150`, `Recovery.tsx:138`).

### H7. Confirmation dialogs are generic, untitled, and not red on Android — High

**Where.** `mobile/src/ui/confirm.ts:6-11`: `Alert.alert('', message, …)` with
`style: destructive ? 'destructive' : 'default'`; generic label `common.confirm` « Confirmer »
(`wifi.tsx:208`, `recovery.tsx`, `account.tsx`).

**What the user experiences.**
- Every write opens the same untitled system alert: blocking, renaming a Wi-Fi network, approving a
  co-owner, taking over the household.
- On Android, the target platform, the « destructive » style is **ignored**, so « Oui, couper
  l'accès » looks exactly like « Annuler »
  ([React Native, Alert](https://reactnative.dev/docs/alert): `style` is iOS only).
- The bodies are long, conditional sentences (e.g. `fr.ts:422`, `fr.ts:650`).

**Research.**
- Confirm only serious actions, name the consequence, label buttons with the action, and prefer
  undo where possible ([NN/g, Confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)).
- Material 3: snackbars with an action should not time out
  ([Android, Compose Snackbar](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/Snackbar.composable)).
  This applies if undo replaces some dialogs.

**Recommendation.**
- Replace `Alert.alert` with a Paper `Dialog`: title = the action (« Bloquer Télé du salon ? »), one
  short consequence line, a red filled action button with icon, and « Annuler » as a text button.
- Keep confirmations for the irreversible or self-affecting actions: take over, delete account, cut
  the whole house, turn off a network.
- Use undo for frequent reversible ones (block, authorise), as RFC-007 D5 plans.
  docs/mobile/README.md ("Every write is confirmed in a dialog") must then be amended.

### H8. Changing the Wi-Fi password: no preview, no warning, no next step — High

**Where.** `mobile/src/app/(tabs)/wifi.tsx:64-86` and `:141-181`; `fr.ts:180`, `fr.ts:191-195`.

### M7. Small touch targets — Medium

**Where.**
- `mobile/src/app/notifications.tsx:73`, `:127`, `:167`, `:279`: `IconButton size={18}`. Paper v5
  draws it as `size + 2 × 8 = 34 dp`
  (`node_modules/react-native-paper/src/components/IconButton/IconButton.tsx:22,156`).
- Compact chips (24 dp minimum height) for states and the journal filter (`StateMarks.tsx:27`,
  `journal.tsx`).
- `compact` buttons for « Approuver » in the inbox (`notifications.tsx:44-55`).

**What the user experiences.** The « × » that deletes a notification (with no undo) sits beside the
row that opens the device. A slip deletes the alert. Old or cracked screens make it worse

([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)).

**Recommendation.** Use the default IconButton size (24 dp icon gives a 40 dp target; add a
`hitSlop` to reach 48). Make the inbox actions regular buttons. Add undo to « supprimer cette
notification » and « Tout effacer ».

### M8. French-only, text-only, typing-only — Medium

**Where.** `mobile/src/i18n/index.ts:9-15` (one locale); « Signaler un problème » requires typed text

- Short term:
  - « Signaler par message vocal WhatsApp » next to the text form.

    playing a recorded Wolof and French explanation. The tutorial videos already in progress can
    feed it.
  - Rename « Plainte » to « Demande ».
- Medium term: a Wolof locale for the core screens. The i18n layer already isolates every string.

### L1. Dimmed text is just under AA contrast in light mode — Low

the case to the contrast test.
